# Gameplay Prototype

`Gameplay Prototype` means the game has crossed the line from idea to playable mechanic. A reviewer can test the intended interaction directly. The project is no longer mostly conceptual. However, the surrounding product layers remain too rough, thin, or unstable for confident public distribution.

This is often the first gate where meaningful comparative review becomes possible, because the reviewer can finally say whether the core action is promising, weak, or misaligned with the intended genre.

## What is usually true at this gate

- the main mechanic or loop can be played repeatedly
- the player can feel the intended verb set, such as jump, dodge, shoot, place, merge, draft, or navigate
- onboarding, presentation, progression, and retention are still limited or rough
- the creator is proving feel, readability, and loop value more than content scale

## What is not yet true

- the product still does not feel complete enough for a broad audience
- major edge cases, UX roughness, metadata gaps, or stability issues are still common
- the game may have promise, but the promise is not yet convincingly delivered end to end

## Comparison discipline

Compare against the closest genre peers in terms of raw interaction quality.

Examples:

- a movement prototype should be compared against relevant movement-heavy peers
- a card-combat prototype should be compared against clarity and turn comprehension in strong card battlers
- a survival-action prototype should be compared against readability and pressure escalation in the arcade survivors space

At this gate, the core question is not content quantity. It is whether the mechanic is already legible and promising enough to justify deeper investment.

## Review expectations

- gameplay and controls matter heavily
- FTUE still matters because the reviewer needs to reach the loop cleanly
- art and audio may still be early, but cannot sabotage evaluation
- store listing should not oversell the state of the build

## Common misclassification risk

Do not call a build `Demo` just because it is playable for several minutes. If the product still mainly exists to validate the mechanic rather than to present a coherent player-facing slice, it is still a `Gameplay Prototype`.

## Feedback framing

At this gate, good feedback focuses on:

- whether the loop earns a second play
- what makes the mechanic hard to read or trust
- what minimum UX and feedback upgrades are needed to let the mechanic shine
- which product layers should be postponed until the loop is genuinely working
