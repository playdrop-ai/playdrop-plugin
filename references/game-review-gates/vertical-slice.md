# Vertical Slice

`Vertical Slice` means the submission demonstrates the intended quality bar across the core parts of the product. Breadth may still be limited, but what exists feels representative rather than provisional. The game is saying, honestly, "this is the quality target we are building toward."

This is a very important gate because it changes the review conversation. At this point, the reviewer is no longer only asking whether the game works. They are asking whether the quality target itself is strong enough.

## What is usually true at this gate

- the core loop, presentation, and flow are coherent together
- the player can imagine the full game without needing to invent the quality bar
- art, UX, and gameplay are aligned enough to read as one product
- the slice is persuasive even if total content is still limited

## What is not yet true

- the game may still lack total breadth, long-tail content, retention systems, or live polish
- balance may still need significant work
- supporting systems may still be incomplete as long as the representative bar is visible

## Comparison discipline

Compare against strong mid-production slices and early access titles that already show their intended quality floor. Benchmark not only against genre leaders, but against games that successfully convinced players and publishers at this same stage.

Ask:

- does this slice show a credible quality target?
- is the game already better than the average slice in its category, or is it still generic?
- what feels representative, and what still feels temporary?

## Review expectations

At this gate, more dimensions should already be solid:

- the loop should be clearly working
- controls should feel intentionally tuned
- UX should stop getting in the way
- visuals should support a distinct identity
- listing honesty should be strong because the product is close enough to judge seriously

## Common misclassification risk

Do not call a build `First Release` just because the slice is polished. A vertical slice can be excellent and still not be broad or durable enough to serve as a real public release.

## Feedback framing

Feedback should now shift toward scale, consistency, and durability:

- what parts are already clearly at the target bar
- what systems would break if the game were scaled up today
- what content or progression work is needed to turn the slice into a product, not just a pitch-quality sample
