Each game is created and judged against these qualities, ordered from highest to lowest priority. Aim to maximize every score. Score all ten dimensions independently. Use priority only to resolve tradeoffs and ties. Listing, Marketing, and Platform Integration are PlayDrop-specific and intentionally lower priority than the seven general game qualities above them.

Request Fidelity judges whether the requested game was built; the other dimensions judge how well it works. Correct, immediate, visible state changes belong to Controls; their visual polish and supporting audio belong to Presentation. Visuals carry far more weight than audio: audio cannot compensate for weak visuals, and missing audio is only a minor issue unless specifically requested or required by play. Core Loop judges moment-to-moment play; Engagement judges whether it sustains interest over time. Listing judges accurate discovery information and preview readiness; Marketing judges persuasive media; Platform Integration judges PlayDrop hosting and relevant capabilities.

- **1 Broken:** Missing, unusable, or fundamentally failing.
- **2 Weak:** Present, but major problems harm the player experience.
- **3 Functional:** Works adequately, with clear quality limitations.
- **4 Strong:** Works well, with only minor weaknesses.
- **5 Excellent:** Exceptional and hard to improve within the intended scope.

**Request Fidelity:** Delivers the requested experience and intended player fantasy.
- **1:** The defining request or player fantasy is absent or unplayable.
- **2:** The concept is recognizable, but major requirements are missing.
- **3:** The core request is delivered with noticeable omissions or compromises.
- **4:** The full request and fantasy are delivered with minor deviations.
- **5:** The request is realized completely with compelling genre-specific detail.
- **Don't:** Hide scope cuts; present them as next-step suggestions.

**Core Loop:** Creates meaningful actions, decisions, challenge, and outcomes.
- **1:** No playable action-and-outcome loop exists.
- **2:** The loop exists, but success and failure do not clearly follow from player actions.
- **3:** The loop works, but offers limited tension, choice, or satisfaction.
- **4:** Repeated actions create clear decisions, fair challenge, and satisfying outcomes.
- **5:** Every action, decision, challenge, and outcome reinforces the experience.
- **Do:** Give the loop designed pressure through stakes, a timer, difficulty, or mastery. Endless loops still need tension, failure, and restart.

**Controls:** Makes player actions responsive, observable, and consequential.
- **1:** The primary action is unavailable or unusable.
- **2:** Inputs register, but responses are unreliable, delayed, or contradict the actual game state.
- **3:** Core actions work reliably but lack precision, clarity, or tuning.
- **4:** Inputs feel responsive, predictable, readable, and genre-appropriate.
- **5:** Tuned game feel makes the core action enjoyable in itself.
- **Do:** Keep controls and overlays aligned with gameplay at every scale.
- **Do:** Make movement, hits, collection, wins, losses, and restarts visibly change the game state immediately.

**Presentation:** Unifies visual art, animation, interface, feedback, and supporting audio.
- **1:** Missing or unreadable presentation prevents understanding the game.
- **2:** Inconsistent assets or missing feedback make the game feel unfinished.
- **3:** Presentation is coherent and readable but generic, sparse, or uneven.
- **4:** Art direction, motion, audio, and feedback strongly support play.
- **5:** A distinctive audiovisual identity gives the game memorable character.
- **Do:** Fill the surface without distortion, letterboxing, dead zones, page scroll, or card framing.
- **Don't:** Park the game title on the play surface outside a short preview or menu state.
- **Do:** Make UI deliberate and coherent with the art direction, never default engine placeholders.
- **Do:** Use visible, correctly scaled, coherent real assets that belong to the background and lighting. Prefer a simple coherent set to a large mismatched one.
- **Do:** Make 3D characters face the direction of travel and visually validate their skeletal animations.

**Usability:** Makes onboarding, navigation, information, and recovery clear.
- **1:** A player cannot reliably start, understand, or recover the game.
- **2:** Goals, interface, or basic flows frequently confuse the player.
- **3:** Onboarding, HUD, navigation, and restart work with noticeable friction.
- **4:** First-time play is clear, information is legible, and recovery is fast.
- **5:** The complete experience feels self-teaching and effortless to operate.
- **Do:** Keep the HUD minimal, safe-area aware, and instantly readable. Avoid instructional sentences parked on screen.
- **Do:** Center dialogs and overlays within the safe area.
- **Do:** Start on a designed frame and reach core gameplay within one input. Avoid blank space, loading leftovers, default engine chrome, and an off-scale canvas.

**Engagement:** Sustains interest through pacing, variety, mastery, or discovery.
- **1:** Interest collapses before the intended experience becomes meaningful.
- **2:** Repetition or weak pacing quickly exhausts the experience.
- **3:** The intended session remains engaging despite limited variety or progression.
- **4:** Pacing, variation, and mastery make continued play attractive.
- **5:** Play stays compelling past the intended session and invites replay.

**Stability:** Runs smoothly and reliably throughout ordinary play.
- **1:** Startup, crashes, hangs, or softlocks prevent normal play.
- **2:** Frequent errors, stutter, or lost progress disrupt the session.
- **3:** Normal play completes reliably despite occasional visible issues.
- **4:** Startup, gameplay, transitions, and restart remain consistently smooth.
- **5:** Performance and reliability remain effectively flawless under sustained play.

**Listing:** Presents the game accurately and clearly for discovery.
- **1:** The listing is missing, unusable, or materially misleading.
- **2:** Major metadata, identity, surface, or discovery problems remain.
- **3:** The listing is complete and accurate but generic or weakly positioned.
- **4:** Clear, specific metadata helps the right players understand and find the game.
- **5:** Exceptional positioning communicates the game's value instantly and truthfully.
- **Do:** Make the game previewable, with a representative first interaction that quickly shows how it plays.
- **Do:** Keep the title, description, tags, surfaces, and identity art accurate to the current build.

**Marketing:** Sells the real game fantasy with compelling video and images.
- **1:** Identity media or task-required marketing media is missing, unusable, or materially misrepresents the game.
- **2:** Weak, inconsistent, or unclear media fails to sell the experience.
- **3:** Media is accurate and serviceable but generic or lacking a strong hook.
- **4:** Cohesive identity art, screenshots, or video clearly communicate the game's appeal.
- **5:** Distinctive, campaign-ready creative makes the real game immediately desirable.
- **Do:** Create purpose-built marketing images and edited gameplay video around the game's real selling points; raw screenshots and unedited recordings are source material, not finished marketing.

**Platform Integration:** Uses relevant PlayDrop capabilities.
- **1:** Broken or missing integration prevents the expected hosted experience.
- **2:** Major SDK, lifecycle, surface, or service issues harm normal use.
- **3:** SDK initialization and lifecycle work correctly but relevant platform capabilities add little beyond basic hosting.
- **4:** Relevant PlayDrop capabilities meaningfully improve play, persistence, competition, sharing, or creation.
- **5:** Deep, reliable integration feels native and materially strengthens the game.
- **Do:** Use PlayDrop capabilities only when they meaningfully improve the game; feature stuffing earns no credit.
