# Safety / Age Rating / Compliance

This dimension protects the platform, players, and creators. It is not optional and it is not soft. The question is whether the game is safe, honest, age-appropriate, and operationally acceptable for distribution on Playdrop. A submission can be rough and still safe. It cannot be unsafe and still be acceptable.

## Comparison discipline

This dimension is less about genre excellence and more about policy, trust, and platform norms. Still, comparisons matter in one way: look at how legitimate games in the same category handle sensitive content, permissions, monetization messaging, external links, and player expectations.

## What to inspect

- deceptive or manipulative behavior
- abusive, hateful, sexual, self-harm, scammy, or malicious content
- misleading monetization or fake system dialogs
- inappropriate age framing
- unsafe links or trust violations
- listing honesty relative to actual content

## Evidence to gather

- inspect both listing and runtime
- note whether trust issues are explicit, implied, or structural
- record any content that should alter age-rating expectations
- call out patterns that look intentionally deceptive rather than accidentally sloppy

## Questions to answer

- Could this submission harm, mislead, or exploit players?
- Is the age framing accurate to what the player will actually see?
- Are there scams, malicious redirects, credential prompts, fake installs, or other abusive behaviors?
- Does the game use manipulative tactics that would be unacceptable on Playdrop?

## Age-rating review lens

Do not reduce this dimension to obvious extreme content only. Age-rating quality also depends on framing and frequency.

Look at:

- explicit violence versus implied violence
- sexual content versus suggestive tone
- intensity, not only category
- whether the listing or marketing hides content that materially changes who the game is appropriate for

If the creator is underselling sensitive content, that is a trust problem even when the game is not otherwise malicious.

## Common failure modes

- hidden mature or disturbing content not reflected in the listing
- deceptive reward language or fake urgency
- misleading UI patterns resembling system prompts
- off-platform flows that are not explained honestly
- social or chat features without appropriate moderation or framing

## Stage-aware interpretation

Stage does not reduce the safety bar. A prototype is not allowed to be deceptive just because it is early. If this dimension fails severely, the result can and should be `Blocked`, even if other dimensions show promise.

## What strong feedback looks like

- "The build contains user trust problems, not just rough edges. The external prompt presentation is misleading enough that it should block normal distribution."
- "The violence level may still be acceptable, but the current age framing likely understates it and should be corrected in the listing."

The goal is precision. Name the issue clearly enough that an operator, creator, or policy reviewer could act on it without guessing.
