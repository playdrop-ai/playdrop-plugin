# Audio / Feedback

Audio is both sensory polish and gameplay communication. This dimension covers music, effects, mix, and the broader feedback layer that helps the player understand success, danger, timing, impact, and pacing. Many games underestimate how much better a loop feels when audio confirms the player's actions clearly.

## Comparison discipline

Compare against games with similar rhythm, pacing, and feedback demands.

- action and arcade games usually need stronger effect clarity and impact
- puzzle and tactics games often need more restrained but highly legible cues
- cozy or ambient experiences may rely more on tone and less on impact, but still need feedback where player state changes matter

The comparison is not "does this have a licensed soundtrack." It is "does the feedback layer support the experience as well as credible peers do?"

## What to inspect

- input confirmation
- reward and failure cues
- damage and danger readability
- music fit and loop fatigue
- mix balance between music, effects, and voice
- whether silence is intentional or just unfinished

## Evidence to gather

- play with sound enabled whenever possible
- trigger the same important interactions repeatedly
- note whether hits, pickups, state changes, failures, and rewards sound distinct
- inspect whether the audio mix helps tension and clarity or muddies both

## Questions to answer

- Does the player hear what just happened?
- Do important actions sound appropriately important?
- Does the game use audio to reinforce timing, threat, or reward?
- Is the soundtrack supporting the fantasy or distracting from it?
- Does weak feedback make the game feel cheaper or less readable than it should?

## Common failure modes

- almost no sound feedback on key actions
- one or two repeated effects causing fatigue quickly
- music that overpowers necessary cues
- inconsistent volume levels between menus and play
- impact-heavy gameplay with weak or placeholder audio that makes actions feel soft

## Stage-aware interpretation

At very early stages, missing soundtrack content may be acceptable. Missing feedback on core actions is much less acceptable. By `Demo` and later, audio should already reinforce the loop. By `Mature Live Version`, weak feedback is a major polish gap.

## What strong feedback looks like

- "The combat would feel much stronger with clearer hit confirmation. Right now actions land visually, but the feedback layer is well below the bar set by comparable run-based action games."
- "The game is intentionally quiet, and that works, but the absence of a distinct fail-state cue hurts comprehension more than the silence helps tone."
