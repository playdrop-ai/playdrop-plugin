# First Time User Experience

FTUE is the quality of the player's first complete contact with the game, from listing expectation to launch to the first few meaningful minutes of play. It is not only tutorial quality. It includes loading, framing, sign-in friction, clarity of objective, pacing of information, and whether the player feels invited or rejected by the opening.

## Comparison discipline

Compare to games with similar complexity and audience assumptions.

- simple arcade or party games should reach comprehension almost immediately
- strategy or system-heavy games can teach more slowly, but must still establish direction and confidence
- social or session-based games should make entry cost feel justified by the promise

Strong references differ by genre, but the shared pattern is clear: the first session should quickly answer "what is this, what do I do, and why should I care?"

## What to inspect

- listing-to-launch continuity
- loading and setup friction
- initial affordances
- clarity of first objective
- timing and density of tutorial information
- whether the game earns the player's patience before asking for effort

## Evidence to gather

- start from a cold session
- do not rely on prior knowledge from earlier runs
- observe the first point where a new player could become confused, bored, or skeptical
- measure how much non-play time happens before the first meaningful action

## Questions to answer

- Does the player know how to begin?
- Does the player understand the goal and primary controls within the first 30 seconds?
- Does the game explain only what is needed, when needed?
- Does early failure teach or merely punish?
- Is the first reward or proof of value delivered soon enough?
- Do auth requirements, permissions, or setup steps feel justified?

## Common failure modes

- too much exposition before agency
- no exposition where clarity is needed
- no discoverable path to perform the primary action
- weak early reward cadence
- opening minutes that look unfinished relative to the listing promise
- tutorial overlays that interrupt rather than support flow

## Stage-aware interpretation

A `Gameplay Prototype` can have minimal onboarding, but the player still needs enough context to test the loop. A `Demo` should already feel intentional in the first session. By `First Release`, first-session confusion is a serious distribution problem, not a minor polish issue.

If the player cannot discover or execute the primary action in a normal first session, FTUE is capped at `3/10`. Do not compensate with credit for menus, labels, or attractive framing.

## What strong feedback looks like

- "The game asks for patience before it proves value. The first meaningful decision arrives too late compared with the strongest games in this session-length category."
- "The tutorial text explains mechanics, but the first objective is still unclear. A stronger FTUE would let the player infer the goal through the first encounter rather than through instructions alone."
